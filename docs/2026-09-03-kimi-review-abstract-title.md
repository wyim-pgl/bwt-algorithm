**Findings**

**1. Title — FM-index attribution. Severity: HIGH.**
Offending phrase: "FM-index seeding for wide-period-range tandem repeat detection."
The title makes a tool-name claim, so a casual reading is charitable: it names what the tool does, not what causes the cheapness. But in context — Motivation paragraph explicitly framing period-range cost as the problem, then an Abstract whose Results sentence opens with "BWTandem seeds candidates from FM-index queries" immediately before the cost numbers — the juxtaposition does the causal work without stating it. The prior review found a causal attribution no experiment established; this text has moved the attribution from the sentence level to the paragraph level. That is the same claim with the verbs removed. Minimal fix: state the actual source of sublinearity where it belongs. The paper's own evidence says wide-range cost is cheap because the algorithm's runtime is dominated by alignment of confirmed seeds, not index lookups; if that's so, the honest framing is "alignment-gated" or "seed-and-extend", and the title could stay only if the text explicitly says the index is not the cost driver. Given the sorted-k-tuple-table equivalence result, "FM-index seeding" describes the implementation, not the mechanism, of wide-range cheapness — but nothing in these two passages says that, so the title still implies a load-bearing property the authors disproved in their own supplement. Fix: add one sentence in the Abstract or Discussion-anchoring line — "a sorted k-tuple table produces identical output; the FM-index is an implementation choice, not the source of range insensitivity" — or retitle to what is actually claimed ("seed-and-align detection" or similar). As is: reworded, not fixed.

**2. Motivation's ULTRA maize number. Severity: HIGH.**
Offending phrase: "one maize widening, 6 to 500 bp, raised ULTRA's runtime from 0.23 to 34.7 h while TRF's stayed flat."
Three problems. (a) It is a competitor's worst case, not replicated by the authors' own architecture, presented as the problem statement, then the authors' range ratio (1.3–1.4×) is measured on human, four threads, a different genome, and — by the paragraph's own admission — the ULTRA pair "comes from a different genome and thread allocation." Using it as the Motivation's lead concrete fact while burying all four qualifications in the Results paragraph is framing by juxtaposition. (b) "Users must bound in advance what a scan should discover" is a stronger claim than the cited datum supports: it is one tool's behavior on one genome. (c) "while TRF's stayed flat" already shows the problem is implementation-specific, not intrinsic — so the Motivation implicitly overstates the generality it needs. Fix: keep the number but attach the caveat at first mention, e.g. "in one tool's worst case," name TRF's flatness as evidence that cost is a design consequence, and drop or soften "Users must bound" to match: "in some implementations, cost scales unpredictably with the period bound." That would make the framing fair. Currently it stacks the deck, then the range-cost paragraph quietly adds the thread/genome mismatches only when defending its own measurement — the asymmetry of caveats (competitor's number uncaveated in the Abstract, own numbers heavily caveated in Results) is the tell.

**3. Abstract carries no range-cost qualification. Severity: HIGH.**
Offending phrase: "The proposition is a single wide-range pass at lower measured compute cost" — and more broadly the entire Abstract's silence on the Tier-3 fixed-window caveat.
The Results paragraph concedes the 1.40× is the cost of *reporting* a wider range, with the actual search unchanged. The Abstract then uses this number ("lower measured compute cost", the 1–2,000 bp wide-range claim) with no trace of that qualification. The prior review's finding was exactly this; the fix in the Results paragraph is genuine, but the Abstract still lets a reader conclude BWTandem's *search* is range-insensitive, which the authors' own architecture section contradicts (Tier 3 always searches 100 bp–100 kb). This is overclaiming by omission, and omission in an Abstract is where it counts. Fix: one clause in the Abstract — "whose range cost, measured as reporting cost under a fixed-window architecture, is 1.3–1.4×" — or drop the range-cost claim from the Abstract entirely and leave it to the body. Results-paragraph qualification alone is not enough; Abstracts are read standalone and quoted standalone.

**4. "lower measured compute cost" vs. "not range-matched". Severity: HIGH.**
Offending phrase: "The proposition is a single wide-range pass at lower measured compute cost."
The sentence pairs two claims whose evidence is disjoint: the cost numbers come from runs the same Abstract flags as "different FASTA scopes; not range-matched" (human runs vs ULTRA capped at 100 bp, different FASTA scopes), and the only range-matched cost data (12.6 h vs 29.8/33.7 h) are admitted not range-matched. "Lower measured compute cost" aggregates a 2.4–2.7× advantage across tools running different inputs, different period ceilings, and self-confessed scope differences. "Measured" is doing heavy lifting — it was measured, just not comparably. The sentence is technically true only in the vacuous sense that something was measured. Fix: delete "lower measured compute cost" or rewrite as "lower measured compute cost in non-range-matched cross-tool runs," which exposes how little that buys — or simply report the two facts and let the reader draw it. Also: "non-leading shared-range accuracy" is admirably frank and makes the surrounding puffery stand out worse.

**5. 1.30–1.41 against 1.20 within-arm spread. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Offending phrase: "the three 1-100 bp runs span 5 h 30 m to 6 h 37 m, a factor of 1.20, against the 1.33 mean between-arm ratio."
The authors deserve some credit for publishing the noise, but they then underweight it. With n=3 pairs and no test, "give ratios of 1.30, 1.30 and 1.41" plus "the same sublinearity" — three points cannot establish sublinearity of *anything*; sublinearity is a claim about the cost-vs-range curve, and a paired run at exactly two period ceilings has no curve at all. At best they have: "at these two ceilings, cost ratio ≈1.3." The text half-concedes this ("what makes the ratio interpretable... because the within-arm spread is itself large") but the word "sublinearity" survives, and the Abstract inherits the stronger reading. Also note the ratios are of *requested/reportable* range — by their own Tier-3 caveat, the true *searched* range ratio is ~1.0, so the between-arm "effect" being only 1.1× the noise band is unremarkable; most of the 7h23m run is shared search. Fix: replace "sublinearity" with "a 1.3–1.4× paired runtime ratio at a twentyfold wider reportable range," and delete any implication of a scaling law. The conclusion drawn ("twentyfold wider range for 1.40× the runtime") is fine if prefixed everywhere with "reportable."

**6. ULTRA 83-fold vs 151-fold normalisation. Severity: MEDIUM.**
Offending phrase: "an 83-fold range increase (ratio of maximum periods) for a 151-fold cost increase."
The parenthetical "(ratio of maximum periods)" is a defensive move that doesn't survive scrutiny. Ratios of maxima ignore floors: widening 6→500 is 83×, but 6 bp is a degenerate minimum nobody runs; the authors' own arm used 1–100, and their own range ratio is quoted as "twentyfold" (1–100 vs 1–2000). If you applied their normalisation to ULTRA symmetrically, max-period ratio is the wrong axis in both directions — and conversely, by their own measure ULTRA's 6→500 against BWTandem's 1–100→1–2000 (20×) should be compared on equal footing: 500/6=83 but 2000/100=20; the asymmetry of the comparison genomes (maize vs human) is flagged, but the asymmetry of the *metrics* is not. Also: 34.7/0.23=151 and 500/6=83 — both numbers involve a 6 bp floor that makes any ratio look extreme. Picking "83-fold" as the denominator is a rhetorically *favourable* choice for BWTandem's side of the contrast. Fix: normalise both sides identically and state the floors, e.g. "widening the maximum period 83-fold cost ULTRA 151-fold; our reporting cost grew 1.3–1.4× at a 20-fold wider reportable ceiling" — and note that ULTRA's 6 bp arm is a near-null baseline, so the ratio is driven by the denominator. Then the comparison is honestly indicative.

**7. Other attackable material.**

- HIGH — Offending phrase: "a blinded single-reader audit supported 4 of 400 BWTandem-only calls." A 1% validation rate is stated without saying what the 400 were sampled from (BWTandem-only calls), and "supported 4 of 400" lets a skimmer read it as a 1% rate of... something, with no denominator of total BWTandem-only calls. If the 400 are all or a sample of the singleton-only set, the "predominantly over-calls" conclusion rests on 4/400 = 1% supported, but the reader can't compute a precision estimate (no sampling fraction, no CI, single reader). Fix: state the sampling frame and give the interval, e.g. "supported 4 of 400 audited" is honest but should also state what fraction of BWTandem-only calls the 400 represent.

- HIGH — Offending phrase: "AniAnn's covers 22.9 points more, at boundary offsets two orders of magnitude coarser." Undefined here. Fix: name the comparator and the metric source in the sentence.
