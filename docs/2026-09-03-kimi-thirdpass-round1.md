Third-pass review of manuscript.md:17–42. Findings ranked most damaging first. All five lens questions yielded at least one finding; none is a null result.

1. **Severity: HIGH — Location: manuscript.md:42 — The tool census does not reconcile.**
   "We benchmarked three genomes against seven tools, six of which produced reported results; TideHunter was excluded for runtime without a completed run… bringing the reporting comparison to eight." The passage names exactly five pre-2026 comparators that produce numbers anywhere in it: TRF, ULTRA, mreps, tantan, TRASH. NCRF is discussed (line 34) only as a tool that "requires motifs to be specified a priori"; the passage never states NCRF was run, and no NCRF result appears. So the sixth reporting tool is unidentifiable from the text: either NCRF was benchmarked and the manuscript never says so, or the count is wrong (5 + 2 = 7, not 8). A reviewer counting on their fingers will land here within minutes, and it is exactly the kind of number that gets silently patched between versions — which is how the discrepancy likely arose.
   **Fix:** name the sixth reporting tool explicitly in line 42 ("seven tools — TRF, ULTRA, mreps, tantan, TRASH, NCRF and TideHunter — of which all but TideHunter produced reported results"), or correct the count. If NCRF was run, one sentence in Section 2 saying with what motifs is mandatory.

2. **Severity: HIGH — Location: manuscript.md:34 vs 21 vs 42 — Three unreconciled BWTandem GRCh38 recall figures.**
   Line 21: "BWTandem reaches 78.87% at its whole-genome configuration… or 81.60% at a permissive setting." Line 34: longdust recovers "half the catalog regions BWTandem does on GRCh38 (41.16% versus 80.53%)". Line 42: the operating curve "spans 54.69%… to 81.60%… across four run-time configurations." So 80.53% is neither of the two operating points the abstract names; it is presumably a third configuration or a different denominator (adotto catalog regions vs. scored regions), but the passage never says which. As written, the paper quotes three different headline recall numbers for the same genome within 20 lines, each attached to a different comparison. Not checkable from this passage whether these are genuinely different metrics — but that is the defect: the reader cannot verify the longdust "half" claim against the abstract's own numbers.
   **Fix:** at line 34, parenthetically identify the 80.53% operating point and metric ("at the whole-genome configuration, catalog-region recall"), or footnote the mapping once.

3. **Severity: HIGH — Location: manuscript.md:38 — The twice-corrected TRF/ULTRA sentence is now accurate-sounding but unreadable, and its human caveat is contentless.**
   "…while TRF's cost, we find, is nearly flat in it on maize (5.2 h at a 6 bp maximum against 5.5 h at 500 bp; Tables 3A–3C), yet not on human at wider ranges (Section 2.2), but a user cannot know in advance which case a tool, or a genome, is." Three defects compounded: (a) "nearly flat in it" — the pronoun reaches back across two clauses to "the period range"; (b) "yet not on human at wider ranges (Section 2.2)" gives no human numbers and points to what is presumably a Methods section for a results claim — a caveat that asserts non-flatness without evidence at the point of assertion; (c) "which case a tool, or a genome, is" is a broken aphorism — a case is something a genome *presents*, not something a tool "is." What the sentence is trying to say: TRF's range-cost flatness is genome-dependent and cannot be predicted a priori.
   **Fix:** split and rewrite: "TRF's cost, by contrast, is nearly flat in the requested range on maize (5.2 h at 6 bp versus 5.5 h at 500 bp; Tables 3A–3C) but not on human (Section 3.2; Table X). Since the user cannot know in advance which regime a genome–tool pair falls in, the bound must be set blind." Give the human numbers or cut "yet not on human."

4. **Severity: MEDIUM — Location: manuscript.md:32 vs 38 — Duplicated facts, one copy per correction round.**
   The maize cost series now appears three times in near-identical form: line 19 ("0.23 to 34.7 h… 5.2 to 5.5 h"), line 32 ("ULTRA took 0.23 h at a maximum period of 6 bp, 12.9 h at 200 bp, and 34.7 h at 500 bp, while TRF… was flat, at 5.2 h, 5.5 h and 5.5 h"), and line 38 ("5.2 h at a 6 bp maximum against 5.5 h at 500 bp; Tables 3A–3C"). Lines 32 and 38 are two paragraphs apart and make the same point with the same numbers in different words — the classic signature of a second correction that added its own copy instead of editing the first.
   **Fix:** keep the full five-number series in line 32 (it carries the 12.9 h intermediate point); in line 38 replace the parenthetical with "(Tables 3A–3C)" and nothing else.

5. **Severity: MEDIUM — Location: manuscript.md:21 — Caveats attached to the wrong clause.**
   "(different FASTA scopes; not range-matched)" is appended to "TRF 33.7 h at a 500 bp maximum," so it reads as a caveat about TRF's run specifically; in fact it qualifies the entire three-way runtime comparison in that sentence. Worse, line 42 then repeats the same three runtimes as the paper's "First" demonstrated property with no caveat at all — the qualification exists only in the abstract, exactly where a skimming reader of the body will never see it. Similarly, "restricted the same way its competitors are" is comma-spliced between "78.87%" and "or 81.60%", leaving it ambiguous whether the restriction applies to the first figure, the second, or both; and "(48.44% against 53.66%)" never says whose precision 53.66% is (ULTRA's, presumably — the reader must guess).
   **Fix:** move the scope caveat to govern the whole clause ("none of the three runs is range-matched, and FASTA scopes differ"); at line 42, repeat the one-word admission ("not range-matched") — unfavourable caveats must travel with the claim, not stay behind in the abstract. Spell out "against ULTRA's 53.66%".

6. **Severity: MEDIUM — Location: manuscript.md:23 with 21 — The defaults admission is real but buried, and duplicated.**
   Line 23: "Reproducing these numbers requires the Supplementary Methods S2 configurations; built-in defaults differ." Line 21: "These are pipeline-plus-supplementary-pass results." These are two copies of the same disclosure, and the line-21 copy is the worse one: "These" has no clear referent (the maize sentence? every result in the abstract?), and "pipeline-plus-supplementary-pass" is undefined jargon for an abstract. The substance — headline numbers are not obtainable with shipped defaults — is exactly the disclosure a hostile reviewer hunts for; the authors did disclose it (credit), but one copy sits in the Availability section where it reads as a download note, and the other is orphaned mid-abstract.
   **Fix:** cut the line-21 sentence entirely; move a single plain version into Results: "All figures reported here use the tuned configurations of Supplementary Methods S2; built-in defaults differ." One disclosure, at the point of claim.

7. **Severity: MEDIUM — Location: manuscript.md:42 — Residual arithmetic slip from the first correction.**
   "the regenerated human run covers periods 1–2,000 bp in 12.6 hours… BWTandem and ULTRA used two threads… giving 25.3, 59.6 and 33.7 core-hours." 12.6 × 2 = 25.2, not 25.3. The abstract's "12 h 39 m" (= 12.65 h) is the value consistent with 25.3 core-hours; line 42 was evidently rounded independently and the two corrections now disagree by a rounding step. Trivial to fix, fatal to leave: a reviewer who checks one multiplication will then check all of them.
   **Fix:** write "12.65 hours (12 h 39 m)" or "25.2 core-hours"; make abstract and line 42 use the same rounded value.
