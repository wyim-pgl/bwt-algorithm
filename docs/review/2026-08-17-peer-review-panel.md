## Step 3 — Disagreement resolution (arbitration record)

**D1 — SC-2, existence/severity disagreement: is the missing FM-index ablation a defect?**
*Positions.* EIC (W2): the paper's originality story is "index seeding is why cost is flat"; no configuration in Suppl. Methods S2 disables seeding, so the attribution is inferred throughout, and self-disclosure does not close it. R2: criterion "Algorithmic framing appropriateness" = MEETS, decision-bearing *no*, on the grounds that "Section 4.3 explicitly concedes no ablation isolates seeding; I take that at face value".
*Type.* Severity disagreement (both agree the ablation is absent).
*Resolution — partially for EIC.* The two seats evaluated different surfaces and both readings survive on the evidence. R2's judgement is correct **as scoped to §4.3**, which states plainly "we have not isolated seeding's contribution by ablation" and "a two-genome contrast in one tool cannot" — the Discussion does not overclaim. EIC's judgement is correct **as scoped to the Title and Abstract**, which do carry the attribution: the title names "a Burrows-Wheeler Transform approach", and the Abstract reads "seeds candidate repeats from FM-index queries … *making* reported-range cost nearly range-independent". A hedge in §4.3 does not repair an unhedged causal verb in the Abstract. *Rationale*: expertise-first does not separate these seats (originality is EIC's remit, algorithmic framing is R2's), so the split is resolved on evidence, and the evidence is surface-specific. Minimum remedy therefore attaches to the Abstract/Title, not to §4.3.

**D2 — SC-8, direction disagreement: which panels move to the Supplement?**
*Positions.* EIC (W7): move 1c, 1e, 3A-b, 3B-b, 3C-b, as the authors themselves propose. R2: 1c and 3C-b carry the paper's most distinctive results and should stay ahead of 3A. R3: keep 1b, 1d, 2, 3B-b, 3C-b; the superseded-rule tables 3A and 3B are provenance and belong in the Supplement. R1: abstains on extent, proposes merging 3A/3A-b, 3B/3B-b and 3C/3C-b into single tables with a "rule" column.
*Type.* Direction disagreement on remedy; no disagreement that extent must fall.
*Resolution — adopt R1's merge remedy, which dissolves the conflict.* Merging each `-b` pair into one table with a rule column removes three tables without losing the dual-rule reporting that EIC (Strength 3), R1 (S4) and R3 (Strength 4) all name as a strength, and it keeps in the main text exactly the uniform-rule panels R2 and R3 both want retained. *Rationale*: evidence-first — R2 and R3 are the two seats reading the tables as consumers and they agree on which panels a reader needs; EIC's list was transported from the authors' own proposal rather than argued independently, and R1's remedy satisfies the extent constraint EIC is enforcing. Residual editorial call (which of 1c/1e stays) is left to the author with reasons stated.

**D3 — SC-18, severity disagreement: is the missing BWT prior art decision-bearing?**
*Positions.* R2 (W5): a named predecessor exists (BWtrs, *Genomics* 2010; Pokrzywa 2009; Danek et al. 2012 for approximate repeats), plus the classical suffix-structure repeat literature; DOES_NOT_MEET, decision-bearing. EIC: reference base MEETS and is not decision-bearing, while noting it is "thin on recent index-based repeat-analysis work other than read mappers".
*Type.* Severity disagreement.
*Resolution — for R2.* Expertise-first: literature coverage and primary-source attribution are R2's seated remit, and EIC's own card marks the reference base as thin in precisely the direction R2 documents. EIC's MEETS is scoped to whether the *comparison set* is the one this readership expects, which is a different question from whether *method prior art* is cited. R2 verified all four candidate references against Crossref with DOIs. Minimum remedy is text-only and does not weaken the paper: naming BWtrs and Danek et al. and stating what BWTandem does that they do not makes the narrow novelty claim checkable rather than merely asserted.

**D4 — SC-25, seat-vs-DA existence disagreement: is the moved-window arm a proper negative control?**
*Positions.* R1 (S5, credited as a strength): moving the window to 400–1,000 bp, "which cannot contain 178 or 356", is what distinguishes "the mechanism is periodicity" from "the window was tuned to this satellite". DA (N4): that window contains 712 = 2×356 = 4×178, so it still contains a harmonic of the monomer and cannot discriminate the two readings; the manuscript itself concedes "we did not tabulate the periods the moved-window arm accepted".
*Type.* Existence disagreement, resolvable arithmetically.
*Resolution — partially for DA.* DA's arithmetic is correct and checkable: 400–1,000 bp excludes 178 and 356 but admits 712. The arm therefore remains a valid control against a *literal constant tuned to this satellite* (R1's reading survives at that scope) but is not a control against *harmonic periodicity*, which is the reading the manuscript's own sentence advances ("periodicity at multiples of the monomer"). The paper's untabulated period distribution is the gap that leaves both readings open. This does not retract R1's credit for running the arm at all — no seat disputes that running it was the right instinct.

**D5 — SC-26, seat-vs-DA disagreement on what the replicates establish.**
*Positions.* R1 (S3): the central cost claim is genuinely replicated; three independent pairs, ratios and memory deltas recomputed exactly from the manifest. DA (M1): the *within-arm* spread across the three 1–100 bp replicates is 1.20× against a between-arm mean of 1.334×, re-pairing gives 1.17×–1.57×, and the deposited README declares differences below 29% uninterpretable.
*Type.* Severity disagreement; both computations are arithmetically correct on the same file.
*Resolution — partially for DA.* Both stand. The paired design (each pair on one node, per §3.1) is a real control that R1 is right to credit, and the paper reports it. But the within-arm spread is present in the deposited file, is directly relevant, is unreported, and is the number the authors' own published interpretability floor should be applied to. Requiring its disclosure costs nothing and does not overturn R1's credit; suppressing it would leave the paper publishing a standard it does not apply to itself, which DA states as a narrower fault than not having one.

**D6 — SC-22 nuance, DA withdrawal vs R3's live finding.**
DA raises and withdraws array fragmentation as an already-disclosed limitation. R3 (W3), EIC and R2 do not dispute the disclosure; their finding is that the *post-merge accuracy is never measured*, which disclosure does not supply. No conflict on the evidence — recorded so the DA's withdrawal is not read as opposition to the CONSENSUS-3 item.

**Unresolved dissent.** None. Every conflict above resolved on evidence or on scope; no seat's position was dismissed for lack of confidence, and no conflict was resolved by confidence weighting. Confidence values are transported as scope disclosure only.

### Summary Assessment

BWTandem is a de novo tandem repeat detector that seeds candidates from FM-index queries over sampled k-mers and aligns only confirmed candidates, evaluated on human GRCh38 against the GIAB adotto catalog, on Arabidopsis Col-CEN, and on maize Mo17, against six competitors. The paper's organising claim is not sensitivity but cost structure: widening the reported period range twentyfold costs 1.30–1.41x runtime, so a user need not guess a period ceiling in advance. On fit this is a natural Bioinformatics Genome Analysis paper — TRF, TideHunter, NCRF and TRASH were all published here — and the question it poses is one this readership has, unusually, never had answered directly.

What holds it back is the gap between the framing and what the experiments establish. The range-cost measurement is, by the authors' own account, a measurement of report width rather than search width, because Tier 3 always searches 100 bp–100 kb; no ablation isolates the index's contribution; the competitor cost figures on which the headline speed comparison rests cannot be re-derived; and on accuracy the tool leads nothing it does not itself call a tie. The manuscript is candid about every one of these — remarkably so, to the point where Discussion 4.2 argues against its own aggressive settings. That candour is a genuine strength and makes the paper repairable rather than fatally overclaimed. The contribution is real but narrower than the title and abstract imply, and the revision should say so in those two places.

### Strengths

1. **The range-cost question is well posed and worth a paper**: The Introduction frames a real, unaddressed practical problem — a user must bound a de novo search by the quantity they are trying to discover, and the cost of a generous bound is tool-specific and unpredictable. The ULTRA-versus-TRF maize contrast makes it concrete in one sentence. This is the kind of question a methods venue should want asked. — `text: §1 "on the maize genome ULTRA took 0.23 h at a maximum period of 6 bp"`
2. **Matched-range scoring**: Restricting every tool to periods ≤100 bp, the widest range all of them searched, and reporting the >100 bp stratum separately, is the correct comparison discipline and is rarer in this literature than it should be. — `table: Table 1b`
3. **Both scoring rules reported rather than silently replaced**: Where an earlier scoring rule was found defective, the corrected panel is added beside the original rather than substituted, so a reader can see what the rule change did. This is exactly the behaviour reviewers usually have to demand. — `table: Table 3A-b`
4. **Self-adverse reporting**: The Discussion states where the recall/precision trade buys nothing, and reports the leave-one-out corroboration result that runs against the authors' own tool. Papers that do this are easier to trust on the claims that remain. — `text: §4.2 "BWTandem's out-of-catalog calls are therefore the least corroborated of the three, not the most"`
5. **Configuration transparency**: Supplementary Methods S2 tabulates nine configuration rows and states outright that built-in defaults do not reproduce any reported number — the failure mode that makes most tool benchmarks unreproducible. — `text: Supplementary Methods S2 "reproducing any number in this paper requires setting them explicitly"`

The narrow arm therefore performed the same long-period search and discarded it. Read correctly, the flat curve is a floor (the tool always pays for the long search) rather than a ceiling advantage, and a user who genuinely wants only STRs pays the satellite cost regardless. ### Detailed Comments

#### Journal Fit

Strong and specific. The Genome Analysis section is the right home: the paper's subject (de novo detection of tandem repeats in finished assemblies), its comparison set (TRF, tantan, mreps, ULTRA, NCRF, TRASH), and its evaluation resources (GIAB adotto, Col-CEN, maize Mo17 T2T) are all standard currency for this readership, and four of the six comparators were published in Bioinformatics or Bioinformatics Advances, so the paper enters an existing conversation rather than starting one. The writing register is right for the venue — technical, quantitative, and not oversold in the body. The fit problems are format rather than scope: extent (W7) and the residual editorial note in the file. I see no argument for redirecting this elsewhere.

#### Originality

Moderate, and correctly self-limited in §1 — the authors do not claim the FM-index is new to repeat analysis. What they claim is new is a detector whose cost is predictably insensitive to the requested period range. Two things reduce the weight of that claim as it currently stands. First, the measurement supporting it is of reporting width rather than search width (W1), so the property demonstrated is weaker than the property claimed. Second, nothing in the experiments attributes the property to the index specifically (W2); the pipeline contains a scanning Tier 1, a satellite gap-fill and a catch-all pass, and the reader is given no way to separate their contributions from the seeded tiers'. There is still an original contribution here — the range-cost measurement itself, run across tools, is new information the field lacks — but it is a benchmarking contribution as much as a methodological one, and the revision should let it be that rather than straining it into an architectural claim.

#### Significance

Mixed. The genuinely significant results are: (i) the demonstration that the cost of a wide period range is tool-specific and unpredictable — ULTRA 151-fold for an 83-fold widening, TRF flat on maize but 4.7x and unfinished at 2,000 bp on human — which changes how a careful user should choose parameters regardless of which tool they end up running; (ii) the 101–2,000 bp stratum, where three tools compete at all and BWTandem recovers the most; (iii) Arabidopsis wall clock, 31 minutes against TRF's 131.9 hours, a 259-fold gap that is not a tie by any reading. Against that, the tool does not lead accuracy anywhere (W4), carries the largest de novo memory footprint on both large genomes, and fragments arrays severely enough that the annotation use case in §4.1 requires a post-merge the paper does not provide. My reading is that the paper is significant for what it measures more than for what it introduces, and it would be a stronger paper if it said so.

#### Structural Coherence

Good within the body, weaker at the ends. Introduction, Methods and Results track each other closely, and the Discussion is organised around the claims rather than around the tool. Two coherence problems: the title's "comprehensive" is contradicted by §4.4 (W6); and the Discussion's opening argument (§4.2, recall over precision) is constructed and then largely withdrawn in its own second paragraph, which leaves the reader unsure what the paper is asking them to accept. Leading §4.2 with the two settings where the trade is real, and demoting the general recall argument to a closing sentence, would fix the latter without changing any content. The research question — what does a wide period range cost, and can it be made cheap — is clear, and the Conclusion (§4.5's closing paragraph) does answer it, though it drifts into a claim about T2T assembly accumulation that the authors themselves mark as unquantified.

#### Title and Abstract

The abstract is dense but well built: it leads with the motivation, gives the range-cost number with its measurement basis, and reports the adotto result including the fact that ULTRA is ahead. Reporting a 0.35-point maize lead as "unresolved" in the abstract itself is the right instinct and rare. Two changes: "comprehensive" in the title should go (W6), and the abstract should carry one clause naming the memory footprint and the fragmentation caveat, both of which a reader needs before deciding to run the tool and currently meets only in §4.4. The Motivation paragraph's opening sentence is doing a lot of work and could be split.

#### Conclusion

§4.5 and the closing paragraph align with the research question and, unusually, distinguish what the data support from what the authors expect. The biological observation about periodic flanking sequence at curated maize boundaries is the most interesting forward-looking item in the paper and is properly caveated for the selection effect. The final claim — that the value of a catalog-free tool grows as T2T assemblies outpace repeat libraries — is asserted, and the authors say so; that is acceptable as a closing statement of expectation but should not be the paper's last word on significance without a sentence naming what was actually demonstrated.

### Questions for Authors

1. Can the range-cost claim be supported by an arm in which Tier 3's internal search window is genuinely narrowed, rather than an arm in which its results are filtered out? If not, will you restate the claim as a property of the reporting range?
2. What is the runtime and accuracy of the pipeline with FM-index seeding disabled in Tiers 2 and 3? Without this the paper cannot attribute its cost behaviour to the index.
3. Can the human competitor runs (ULTRA, TRF, tantan) be re-executed under recorded job accounting, so that the abstract's speed comparison rests on one provenance class rather than two?
4. Given a 29% wall-clock difference between two executions of the identical two-thread configuration, why is 12.1 h rather than 9 h 21 m the figure carried into the abstract, and what is the intended reading of the two?
5. Will the released commit be re-run to regenerate at least the Table 1a and Table 1d outputs, so the deposited artifacts and the released code correspond?
6. Does a post-processing merge exist, or could one be added, that would give one interval per satellite array? The annotation use case argued in §4.1 appears to require it.
7. Are the periods BWTandem reports outside the nominal monomer band harmonics of the true period? §4.4 leaves this open, but it determines whether the band-filter losses are a labelling problem or a detection problem.

### Summary Assessment

This is a carefully executed and unusually candid benchmarking study whose confound register is more complete than most papers in this literature — unequal search ranges, unequal thread counts, two different input FASTA files, inherited cost provenance and corroborator asymmetry are all stated in the text rather than discovered by a reviewer. My independent checks support that impression: every call count in Tables 1a, 1b and 1d matches the deposited output files exactly, the corroboration arithmetic of §4.2 closes to within 0.02 points when recomputed from raw precision, and the three replicate range-cost pairs reproduce the reported ratios and memory deltas exactly.

The design nonetheless has one gap the manuscript does not close and one it closes in only one direction. The gap is parameter selection: the nine BWTandem configurations were chosen by scoring candidate settings against the same benchmark used for evaluation, while every competitor ran at defaults, and the manuscript discloses neither the procedure nor the asymmetry. The one-directional closure is Table 1b, designated as the panel on which base-pair metrics should be read, where BWTandem receives a native re-run and TRF and TRASH receive a post-hoc filter the caption itself calls understating.

Both are repairable within a revision cycle. Neither touches the range-cost claim, which is the paper's strongest and best-evidenced result.

### Weaknesses

1. **W1 — Detection thresholds were selected against the evaluation benchmark, and the selection is not disclosed**: Supplementary Methods S2 gives nine configurations, all of them environment overrides on the tool's defaults, and §2.2.1 says only that they "override the tool's built-in defaults". The manuscript nowhere states how the specific values were arrived at. The deposited artifact answers that question: `exp1_human/loop/ledger.tsv` records 44 candidate configurations scored against an adotto ground-truth subset (`gt_2122_all.bed`, chr21 and chr22), with `pool_rec`/`pool_prec` columns, and the accepted settings reappear verbatim in Supplementary Methods S2 — `CATCHALL_MIN_COPIES=3` (ledger `f_cp3`, chosen over `f_base` at 84.05/50.00 for 82.73/51.57), `CATCHALL_MIN_IDENTITY` at 0.72/0.76, `TIER1_SHORT_PERIOD_MAX=9` with floors 17/17 (`a_spm9f17`), `TIER1_FMSCAN_MIN_DENSITY=0.45` (`p1_fmdens45`), `TIER2_MISMATCH=0.30` (`p1_t2mm30`). Meanwhile §3.1 states "We did not sweep the competitors' own sensitivity parameters", framing this only as a presentational asymmetry (a curve versus points) rather than a tuning asymmetry.

   Why this is a problem: the reported region recall and precision are in-sample estimates of a benchmark-fitted configuration compared against out-of-the-box competitors. This is the pitfall Weber et al. (2019) name as the self-assessment trap, and it bears directly on the paper's headline accuracy comparison ("recovers 79.9% of catalogued regions to ULTRA's 81.6%", Abstract). I want to be precise about the magnitude: chr21 and chr22 are only 48,344 of 1,784,804 catalogued regions on chr1–22,X,Y (2.71%), so this is not gross test-set leakage. The bias operates through configuration fitting to one benchmark's conventions — its interval merging, its region granularity, its 1 bp overlap criterion — and that transfers to every chromosome.

   How to improve: (i) state the selection procedure and the number of configurations evaluated in Methods; (ii) re-report the headline accuracy figures on a held-out chromosome set that excludes chr21 and chr22, so the tuning and evaluation surfaces are disjoint; (iii) either perform a comparable sensitivity sweep for ULTRA and tantan, or state plainly in §3.1 and the Abstract that BWTandem's configuration was benchmark-fitted while the competitors' was not. The authors already have the machinery for (ii) — the scorer takes a chromosome restriction.

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses

Three claims are posed in §1 and each is testable as stated: (1) cost nearly insensitive to the requested period range; (2) no per-genome range tuning needed; (3) run-time-selectable sensitivity. Claim (1) is the strongest and is the only one with replicates. The authors are appropriately careful that (1) is a claim about *reported*-range cost, not searched-range cost, because Tier 3's internal window is fixed at 100 bp–100 kb regardless of `--max-period` — §3.1 states this plainly ("the narrow arm performed the same long-period search and discarded its findings"), which is the honest framing and I credit it. Claim (3) is correctly attributed to an optional periodicity pass rather than to the index.

#### Research Design

The three-genome, seven-tool design is appropriate to the questions and the genome choice is well justified (§4.1 notes, correctly, that these three are among the minority with curated satellite coordinates, which is both why they can serve as ground truth and why they are not the use case the tool targets). The scoping note on GRCh38 versus T2T is exactly right and forestalls a predictable objection.

The design's weakness is asymmetry of effort. BWTandem received nine configurations, a native re-run for the matched-range panel, three replicate pairs, an ablation with a negative control, and a build-fix accounting. The competitors received one run each at defaults, with cost figures inherited from a round whose records are gone. Some of that asymmetry is unavoidable and the authors declare parts of it; the parts that are avoidable (W1, W2) are the ones I would ask them to fix.

#### Sampling Strategy

Not a sampling design in the survey sense. The relevant analogue — which sequence and which catalogue regions enter the evaluation — is handled well: restriction to chr1–22,X,Y is applied to both sides, the accession-to-UCSC conversion is deposited, and the discarded interval counts are reported (tantan's 149,706 out-of-scope calls, §2.2.2). The maize and Arabidopsis truth sets are small (25, 17, 17 arrays; five centromeres), which the paper acknowledges by declining to rank sub-point differences.

One residual concern: the CEN180 truth set is itself a derived product (blastn hits of a 177 bp consensus, filtered from 68,840 to 66,683, with the invocation lost) and is explicitly a conserved subset at ≥80% identity. The paper states this and correctly notes that all rows are scored against the one file, so rankings are safe. Absolute recall figures near 99.7–99.8% should nonetheless be read as recall over the conserved subset, and the Abstract's "recovers 99.7% of Arabidopsis CEN180 monomers" would be more accurate with that qualifier attached.

#### Data Collection

Command lines and versions are deposited for five of seven tools; mreps and NCRF are incomplete and the paper says so. The single-container execution of all competitor runs is good practice. The container is a writable sandbox directory rather than an image, so no checksum exists — declared, but it does mean the competitor environment is not independently reconstructable.

The two-FASTA issue (§2.2.2) is handled about as well as it can be after the fact: accuracy is unaffected because scoring restricts both sides, and the ~5% cost inflation on the competitor side runs against the authors' own interest, which is the right direction for an uncorrected confound to run.

#### Analysis Methods

Metrics are appropriate in kind. The two-level reporting (region and base pair) is the right structure, and computing base-pair metrics on merged intervals while leaving the benchmark side unmerged is stated and consistent. The period-band sensitivity analyses (Tables 3B-b, 3C-b, and the 150–200 / 150–400 bp bands in §3.2) are a genuine methodological contribution — they demonstrate that "coverage under a monomer-length filter" measures period assignment as much as detection, and the authors report the result even though it is unflattering (§4.4: 15.6, 33.0 and 17.8 points of coverage lost under banding, against ≤2.8 for TRF and ULTRA).

The adjusted-precision analysis is the weakest instrument, and the authors say so themselves in §4.2 ("the corroborating tools are de novo detectors responding to the same periodicity signal, not independent evidence"), and further report that the symmetric leave-one-out variant runs against BWTandem. Reporting a metric that does not favour you, in the section where you might have leaned on it, is the correct handling.

The unaddressed analysis gap is the overlap criterion (W4).

#### Statistical Reporting Adequacy (Step 4a)

Items 1–7 of the checklist have no applicable surface: the manuscript reports no hypothesis test, p-value, effect size, confidence interval, power analysis, assumption test or missing-data mechanism, and §2.2.3 declares this explicitly. That declaration is appropriate for deterministic software rather than a reporting omission, and I do not treat it as one. Judgement: `NOT_ASSESSED`.

**Arithmetic recompute (item 8).** No statistic in this manuscript falls inside any of the four bounded procedures. `p_from_test_statistic` requires a test statistic with a test family and df — none is reported. `grim` and `grimmer` require a reported mean (and SD) over an integer-bounded scale with a known N — the manuscript reports proportions and byte/base counts, not scale means, so the integer-product reachability argument has no support to run on. `n_from_df` requires a reported df — none appears. Surfaces checked: Abstract, §2.2.3, Tables 1a–1e, Table 2, Tables 3A–3C and 3A-b/3B-b/3C-b, Supplementary Tables S1–S3. **Attestation: no recomputable statistics under the four bounded procedures.**

Outside those procedures I did verify the manuscript's derived arithmetic by ordinary recomputation, and it is sound. Core-hour conversions (12.1 × 2 = 24.2; 29.8 × 2 = 59.6; 33.7 × 1 = 33.7), ratio claims (12.1/29.8 = 40.6% → "41%"; 2000/100 = 20 → "twentyfold"; 500/6 = 83.3 → "83-fold"; 34.7/0.23 = 150.9 → "151-fold"; 131.9/0.51 = 258.6 → "259 times"; 15.4/0.51 = 30.2 → "thirtyfold"), the range-cost ratio (7:23/5:17 = 1.397 → "1.40") and all three replicate ratios and memory deltas reproduce. The full corroboration chain of Table 1e and §4.2 closes to within 0.02 points for all five tools under both rules, and the distance-class decomposition of §3.1 is consistent with the Table 1d precision figures under an implied benchmark size of ~239 Mb. I found exactly one arithmetic discrepancy in the whole manuscript, W9, and it is a rounding of a range bound by 0.15 GB.

#### Results Presentation

Complete, and unusually so — negative and unflattering results are reported rather than omitted (the 51.7% of bases more than 500 bp from any benchmark region with the pass enabled, §3.1; the leave-one-out corroboration reversal, §4.2; the banding losses, §4.4; the fragmentation scores of 0.00, §3.3.2). Ties are labelled as ties with a stated threshold, and no Discussion claim rests on a sub-point difference — I checked this and it holds.

Selective-reporting risk is low on the reporting axis and concentrated instead on the configuration axis (W1): what is unreported is not a result but the search over configurations that produced the reported one.

#### Reproducibility

The strongest and weakest aspects of the paper sit next to each other here. The manifest design — per-row source file, line count, byte count, first-megabyte hash, job ID, elapsed, cgroup peak, node, scorer, scorer hash, scoring rule, repo commit — is better than nearly anything I see at this venue, and the honesty about what it cannot cover (competitor cost, the lost blastn invocation, the executed-versus-released commit gap) is exemplary. But the artifact is a verification *record* rather than a verification *kit*: the files it points at are not deposited (W6), and the released code does not regenerate the deposited outputs byte-for-byte. Closing that gap is a deposition task, not new science, and it would move this paper from well-documented to genuinely reproducible.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected

- **Selection effect / self-assessment trap** (the checklist's p-hacking analogue for method benchmarking): present and material — W1. Forty-four configurations were evaluated against the benchmark family and the best-performing were reported, with no disclosure and no held-out surface.
- **Confounding**: present in three places — unequal restriction method in the matched-range panel (W2), unrecorded thread allocation behind the core-hour normalization (W3), and asymmetric disclosure of the catch-all configuration difference across genomes (W5).
- **Circular corroboration**: present but explicitly identified and disarmed by the authors (§4.2), so not a defect.
- **Survivorship bias**: partially present and correctly flagged in §3.3.3 — the fourteen measured flanks "are the ones the detector chose to extend into", and the authors state that the matched control on unextended boundaries was not run. They draw a correspondingly hedged conclusion.
- **Overfitting**: this is W1 under another name. There is no held-out evaluation anywhere in the paper.
- **Ecological fallacy, Simpson's paradox, reverse causation, multicollinearity, endogeneity**: not applicable to this design.

### Summary Assessment

This is a carefully written, unusually self-critical methods paper whose engineering claim — that FM-index seeding makes reported-range cost nearly flat in the maximum period — is measured on the tool's own runs and is the kind of result the field needs. The biological framing is largely accurate, and the CEN180/CEN178 reconciliation and the period-assignment caveat are handled better than in most published comparisons. The Discussion withdraws claims that the Results could be read as supporting, which is rare and should be preserved through revision.

The domain problems are in positioning rather than in execution. The comparison set is the one a 2019 paper would have assembled: it omits SRF, HiCAT, StringDecomposer, NTRprism and SatXplor, and the omission is not merely bibliographic, because SRF's existence contradicts a Discussion claim about period bounds. The prior-art paragraph presents a BWT tandem-repeat detector without naming BWtrs (Genomics, 2010) or its approximate-repeat successor. Two comparators are run below their own documentation: TRF without `-l` on genomes with multi-megabase arrays, and tantan at a 100 bp ceiling in three satellite tables after the authors re-ran two other tools for exactly that reason. And the adotto catalog is a polymorphic-TR benchmark, filtered to loci carrying an observed non-SNP variant, which is not the quantity the precision axis is presented as measuring.

None of this is unfixable, and none of it touches the BWTandem-side measurements, which are documented to a standard well above the norm.

### Strengths

1. **CEN180/CEN178/177 bp reconciliation done explicitly**: the paper names the discrepancy, states which name it uses in headers and why, and does not let three numbers for one family pass silently. This is the single most common source of confusion in the Arabidopsis centromere literature and most papers ignore it. — **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §2.2.3 "We use the family's common name CEN180 in column headers; the Col-CEN literature names the family CEN178"`

2. **The period-assignment caveat is stated as a limitation rather than buried**: quantifying that a monomer-length band costs BWTandem 15.6/33.0/17.8 points on knob180/TR-1/CentC against ≤2.8 for TRF and ULTRA is exactly the number a satellite annotator needs before adopting the tool, and most authors would have reported only the unfiltered row. — **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.4 "it covers these arrays as well as any tool that also places sub-kilobase boundaries"`

3. **The above-period-100 stratum is the paper's genuine domain contribution and is reported honestly**: it is the only panel where BWTandem is not dominated, the range-mismatch against TRF's 500 bp cap is stated in the caption rather than elided, and the recall figures are small enough that a lesser paper would have rescaled them. — **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table 1c`

4. **The flank-periodicity measurement is a real biological observation, correctly hedged**: measuring autocorrelation at the family period in the sequence beyond curated maize array boundaries, against a sampled intergenic background with a stated 95th percentile, is the right test, and the selection-effect caveat is the one a referee would have raised. — **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3.3 "median of 0.430, against 0.268 for intergenic sequence sampled on the same chromosomes"`

### Weaknesses

1. **W1 — TRF was run below its own documented configuration for these genomes, and TRF carries two of the paper's headline claims.** Supplementary Table S1 gives `trf FASTA 2 7 7 80 10 50 MAXP -ngs -h`, with no `-l`. The TRF distribution documents `-l <n>` as "the longest TR array expected in the input is at most n million bp long. The default is 2 (for 2 million)", states that "For HG38, a value of 6 is necessary", and gives the developers' own hg38 example as `trf409.linux64.exe hg38.fasta 2 5 7 80 10 50 2000 -l 6`. All three benchmarked genomes contain arrays above the 2 Mb default: Col-CEN centromeres are megabase CEN178 arrays, and the maize knob180/CentC arrays are multi-megabase. TRF is the comparator behind the Abstract's "TRF 97.6% in 131.9 [hours]", the Introduction's TRF-flatness observation, Section 3.2's "259 times the wall clock", Table 1c's above-100 stratum, and the whole of Discussion 4.3, whose "sharpest cost contrast in these experiments is between the two genomes, and it is about TRF" is the paper's mechanistic story. Every one of those is measured on a run configured below the developers' documented requirement, so TRF's runtime may be inflated and its satellite coverage and above-100 recall depressed by the run rather than by the algorithm. Fix: re-run TRF with `-l` set to cover the largest expected array on each assembly (the docs' hg38 value is 6), report both, and if the numbers move, revise the Abstract, Section 3.2 and Section 4.3. Field-norm grounding: the tool authors' own README and usage example, i.e. documented developer practice, not my own expectation.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Supplementary Table S1, TRF row` | **Confidence**: 4 — I run TRF on satellite genomes and set `-l` as routine; I have not re-run these three benchmarks

2. **W2 — adotto v1.2.1 is a polymorphic-tandem-repeat benchmark, and the paper's precision axis is presented as if it were a repeat annotation.** The catalog's own `regions/DataDescription.md` records that regions were "Merged calls from the sources with 25bp of slop added to each end", and that at v0.2 "Using a pVCF created from 172 haplotype-resolved long-read assemblies, we removed any TR region which had no observed non-SNP variant. The manuscript says only that the catalog "is known to be incomplete" and that it is "a region catalog of merged intervals around repeat loci, not a base-resolution repeat annotation". Neither statement conveys that the majority of candidate TR regions were deliberately removed for being monomorphic. The consequence is specific and large: under this target, a perfect tandem-repeat detector cannot approach 100% precision, "recovers 79.9% of catalogued regions" means 79.9% of *variable* TR loci rather than of human tandem repeats, and the entire Section 4.2 argument — including the claim that BWTandem-P is the de novo precision leader among tools recovering more than half the benchmark — is conditioned on a benchmark property the reader is never told. The cross-tool ordering probably survives, since all tools face the same target; the absolute interpretation does not. Fix: state the catalog's construction and the 58% polymorphism filter in Section 2.2.3, and re-word every absolute precision claim as "precision against a polymorphic-TR catalog".

3. **W3 — the comparison set and the survey are pre-2020 for a 2026 satellite paper, and one omission falsifies a Discussion claim.** Section 4.1 states that among de novo detectors "most of them require the user to bound the period range in advance (TRASH being the exception, at 107.6 h on human, Section 1)". SRF (Zhang, Chu, Cheng & Li, *Genome Research* 2023) reconstructs "satellite repeat units and HORs from accurate reads or assemblies without prior knowledge on repeat structures" — no period bound, no motif, k-mer-count based rather than alignment based, and in production use on T2T assemblies. TRASH is therefore not the exception, and the paper's central practitioner argument is stated against a landscape that no longer holds. The same gap runs through the Introduction's survey, which discusses tools up to 2023 and omits the entire HOR/satellite-structure toolset: HiCAT (*Genome Biology* 2023), StringDecomposer (*Bioinformatics* 2020), NTRprism as introduced in the T2T centromere paper (*Science* 2022), SatXplor (*Briefings in Bioinformatics* 2025) and RepeatOBserver (*Molecular Ecology Resources* 2025). 4. **W4 — the CEN180 truth set excludes the divergence regime the Introduction says defines the problem.** The Introduction motivates the whole satellite half of the paper on divergence: "satellites tolerate substantial inter-copy divergence, 20–30% within a CEN180 array (Naish et al., 2021)". ### Detailed Comments

#### Literature Review

- **Coverage**: Two distinct gaps. (i) *Method prior art*: no BWT or suffix-array tandem-repeat detector is cited, though BWtrs (2010) and Danek et al. (2012) exist and the latter targets approximate repeats specifically. The classical suffix-structure repeat literature (Stoye & Gusfield; REPuter; enhanced suffix arrays; TRStalker) is also absent. (ii) *Satellite annotation, 2020–2025*: SRF, HiCAT, StringDecomposer, NTRprism/Altemose, SatXplor and RepeatOBserver are all missing. The tools cited (TRF 1999, mreps 2003, tantan 2011, TideHunter 2019, NCRF 2019, TRASH 2023, ULTRA 2024) form a coherent set but one that stops before the T2T satellite-annotation wave the Introduction itself invokes.
- **Integration quality**: Good where it exists. The Introduction does not enumerate; it organises tools by what they demand of the user (a period bound, a motif, runtime) and builds the gap argument from that axis. That structure is the right one and it survives the additions above — SRF simply becomes a second entry in the "no bound required" column alongside TRASH, and the argument shifts from "nobody does this" to "the alternatives that do pay in runtime or in output form".
- **Research gap argument**: Persuasive in shape, under-evidenced in specifics. The core observation — that the cost of a wide period range is tool-specific and not predictable from documentation — is well made and supported by the ULTRA maize series. It is weakened by the fact that the TRF half of the evidence comes from an under-parameterised run (W1), and by Olson & Wheeler (2024) having already reported TRF failing on T2T chromosomes at some settings, which the paper does not acknowledge as prior observation of its own motivating phenomenon.

#### Theoretical Framework

- **Appropriateness**: The index-versus-scan framing is the right one and is stated with unusual care. Section 4.3's concession that TRF also reads candidate periods from k-tuple spacing, so "the difference is not that the information is unavailable to them", is the correct and honest statement of what an FM-index buys. Locating a query in time proportional to pattern length independent of occurrence count, then paying O(occ) for positions, is accurately described.
- **Application depth**: Applied, not merely named. The three tiers map onto distinct period regimes with stated parameters, the LCP/Kasai use is appropriate, and Supplementary Methods S1.3 gives the realised adaptive values rather than only the formulas. The framework is used to *predict* the range-cost result and then the prediction is tested, which is the right relationship between framing and experiment.
- **Alternative frameworks**: Two are unconsidered. (i) *k-mer counting* — SRF's approach, which reaches satellite units without an index or an alignment, and which the omission in W3 makes invisible. (ii) *Spectral/autocorrelation methods* — NTRprism and RepeatOBserver detect periodicity by transform rather than by seeding. The paper's own supplementary passes (satellite gap-fill, catch-all) are autocorrelation-based, so it has independently arrived at the second family without citing it; naming that convergence would strengthen rather than weaken the paper.

#### Academic Argument Quality

- **Factual accuracy**: Satellite unit lengths are correct throughout (CEN178 178 bp with a ~356 bp dimer, knob180 ~180, TR-1 ~358–359, CentC ~156, alpha-satellite 171/342). The T2T assembly citations are correct and current. Two problems: the TAG/knob framing (W9) and the untraceable divergence figure (W11). One framing problem: the paper describes adotto as merely "incomplete" when its incompleteness is a deliberate polymorphism filter (W2).
- **Argument logic**: Generally tight, and the paper repeatedly declines to draw conclusions its data will not carry — the 0.35-point CentC lead read as a tie, the refusal to claim seeding as the cause of the Arabidopsis/maize inversion without an ablation, the statement that a two-genome contrast in one tool cannot isolate an effect. One logical gap: Section 4.1's period-bound argument is stated over a tool set that excludes the counterexample (W3). One inconsistency: the re-run policy stated in 3.2 does not describe the treatment of tantan (W8).
- **Terminology precision**: The best-handled aspect of the paper. CEN180/CEN178/177 bp is reconciled explicitly; "region recall" versus "base pair recall" versus "monomer recall" are defined separately and their different call sets stated; "reported period" is consistently distinguished from array period, which is the distinction most comparisons in this literature elide. Only the microsatellite bound is doubly defined (W12).

#### Contribution to the Field

- **Incremental contribution**: Methodological and narrow, but real. Specifically, the paper establishes that under an FM-index seeding architecture the wall-clock cost of *reporting* a twentyfold wider period range is 1.30–1.41×, replicated across four measured pairs, where ULTRA's is 151× for an 83-fold widening. That is a genuine advance in a dimension the field has not measured, and the honest qualification — that Tier 3's search window is fixed so the narrow arm did the same long-period work — makes it a claim about architecture rather than about search, which is the correct scope. The secondary contribution is the above-period-100 stratum (Table 1c), the only panel where the tool is not dominated.
- **Positioning**: Under-positioned against 2020–2025 satellite work and over-positioned by the title. "Comprehensive tandem repeat detection" is not what the results support: on the matched range the tool is at or below ULTRA on both axes at three of four operating points, its arrays are heavily fragmented, and its reported periods often fall outside the monomer band. Against TRF and TRASH above period 100, and on cost, it wins.
- **Overclaiming**: Low in the Discussion, which is the paper's strongest section — Section 4.2's "at those settings nothing is being bought" is a sentence most authors would not write. Moderate in the Abstract, where "gives the highest maize CentC coverage by an unresolved 0.35 points" leads with a lead the paper's own Methods declares a tie; the hedge is present but the ordering invites the misreading. The title is the one place I would call it overclaiming outright.

### Minor Issues

### Perspective Review Report (Peer Reviewer 3)

**Manuscript**: BWTandem: a Burrows-Wheeler Transform approach for comprehensive tandem repeat detection in assembled genomes
**Target venue**: Bioinformatics (OUP), Original Paper
**Seat**: 4 of 5 — cross-disciplinary / practical perspective

### Reviewer Identity

I build pangenome and T2T-assembly annotation pipelines: the code that takes a freshly finished haplotype assembly and produces the repeat annotation other people then consume — satellite array intervals, HOR structure, motif libraries, masking tracks — usually across tens to hundreds of assemblies at once. I am not the algorithms reviewer and I do not evaluate the FM-index complexity argument or the statistics; Reviewers 1 and 2 own those. ### Summary Assessment

BWTandem's engineering claim is real and, for my community, genuinely useful: a single pass covering 1–2,000 bp at a cost nearly flat in the requested range, which removes a parameter that in practice silences whole repeat classes. The Arabidopsis result (99.7% CEN180 monomer recall in 31 minutes against ULTRA's 10.1 hours) is the kind of number that changes how often I re-run a survey, and the maize periodic-flank analysis in Section 3.3.3 is a real contribution to how satellite array boundaries get curated.

What I cannot find is the experiment that supports the paper's own positioning. Section 4.1 says the tool is for assemblies "completed without such catalogs" and then concedes that how large that set is "is something we assert rather than measure." Every result in the paper is agreement with a catalog: adotto, CEN180 BLAST coordinates, curated maize arrays. Nothing demonstrates the workflow the tool is for — assembly in, usable family-level annotation out, no prior library. The authors already have the data to run it.

Three practical properties then decide adoption and the paper leaves all three open: the output fragments each satellite array into hundreds of calls (Section 3.3.2), sensitivity can only be changed by re-running rather than by filtering a score, and the reported period is unreliable enough that period-band selection costs up to 33 points of coverage. Those are the three things a pipeline builder does with a repeat BED. I would still adopt this for one narrow job; I could not yet build a pangenome annotation stage on it.

### Strengths

1. **Reports where its own tool buys nothing**: The manuscript states plainly that its aggressive operating points match a competitor's recall at lower precision and therefore purchase nothing, and confines the recall argument to the conservative end and the above-100 bp stratum. From a practitioner's seat this is worth more than another benchmark table: it tells me which configuration to actually run (P, or F if I plan to curate), which most tools papers leave me to discover by burning cluster time. — `text: §4.2 "so at those settings nothing is being bought"`

2. **Reproducibility disclosure at a level that makes adoption possible at all**: The paper states that its numbers require the Supplementary S2 configurations and that built-in defaults differ, tabulates nine configuration rows, and deposits a per-figure provenance manifest. Most tools papers I try to reproduce fail at exactly this step. The tension with parameter burden (W5) is real, but the disclosure itself is a strength, not the problem. — `text: Data and Code Availability "reproducing the reported numbers requires those configurations, since the tool's built-in defaults differ from the benchmarked settings"`

3. **The periodic-flank measurement is the paper's most transferable finding for my community**: Showing that detector extensions past curated maize array boundaries carry the family period, above intergenic background and above the array interiors, bears on how satellite boundaries are defined rather than on any one tool. It is currently a Results subsection and a Future Directions paragraph; it deserves more prominence, with the selection-effect caveat the authors already state. — `text: §3.3.3 "the curated coordinates appear conservative at these loci, and a boundary trimming step would remove sequence carrying the family period"`

4. **Both scoring rules reported side by side rather than the favourable one**: Tables 3A-b, 3B-b and 3C-b re-score the maize experiments under a uniform rule that is in several places worse for BWTandem than the earlier rule (banded CentC coverage falls 17.8 points, banded TR-1 33.0), and the paper recommends the stricter rule to readers. Self-correction of this kind is rare and is directly useful to me, because the banded rule is the one my pipelines apply. — `table: Tables 3A-b, 3B-b and 3C-b`

### Weaknesses

1. **W1 — The paper's design goal is never operationalised; every experiment measures agreement with a catalog.** Section 4.1 identifies the target situation as assemblies completed without curated repeat coordinates, notes that the three benchmark genomes are precisely not that situation, and concedes the size of the target set is asserted rather than measured. That leaves the central practical claim of the Discussion unsupported by any experiment. It matters because "recovers 79.9% of catalogued regions" is evidence about a genome that has a catalog, and says nothing about whether a user with no catalog gets something they can use. **The remedy is cheap and uses data already in hand**: run a catalog-blind arm — take the maize or Arabidopsis assembly, run BWTandem with no template, cluster the reported motifs (any standard clustering), and ask whether the largest clusters recover the CEN180 / knob180 / TR-1 / CentC consensus sequences to some identity threshold and their array coordinates. Report how many curated families are recoverable and how many spurious families of comparable size appear alongside them. That converts Section 4.1's assertion into a measurement and would be the most valuable single addition to the paper.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4.1 "That is the situation a tool of this kind is not built for." "how large that set is relative to the annotated one is something we assert rather than measure"` | **Confidence**: 5 — this is the exact workflow I build, and the absence is stated by the authors themselves

2. **W2 — Detection parameters are a function of the sequence a repeat happens to sit on, with a hard discontinuity at 100 Mb, and the resulting non-invariance to assembly contiguity is never tested.** Tier 3's k-mer size, stride and occurrence cap are computed per chromosome, giving a 90 Mb chromosome three times the occurrence budget of a 250 Mb one, and a separate large-chromosome mode switches on above 100 Mb. For a single reference genome this is a tuning detail. For my use case it is a correctness hazard: in a pangenome, the same satellite array sits on a T2T chromosome in one haplotype and on a 4 Mb unplaced contig in another, and under these formulas the two receive different k-mer sizes and different occurrence caps. Annotation differences driven by assembly contiguity would then be indistinguishable from biological variation between haplotypes. Nothing in the paper bounds this: all three assemblies are chromosome-scale, and the human run was restricted to the 24 primary chromosomes. **Suggested experiment**: partition one maize or Arabidopsis chromosome into 1 Mb, 10 Mb and whole-chromosome pieces, run each, and report call-level concordance; and report throughput and concordance on a realistic fragmented input (a scaffold-level assembly of a genome you already ran). If the output is invariant, that is a selling point worth stating; if it is not, users need to know the invariance boundary.

3. **W3 — Fragmentation makes the satellite coverage numbers hard to consume, and the paper's own aggressive merge already failed to fix it.** Section 3.3.2 reports 253–475 calls per array for BWTandem, a normalised fragmentation score rounding to zero, against TRASH's 0.05, and closes with "an analysis needing one interval per array will have to merge." That merge is not a footnote for my pipelines — it is the deliverable. What makes this more than a cosmetic complaint is Section 2.1.5: post-processing already merges same-family calls across gaps of up to 100× the period, so two calls of a 178 bp monomer separated by 17.8 kb can be joined, and hundreds of calls per array survive that. So the residual fragmentation is not an absent merge step; something structural is defeating an already permissive one, most plausibly the period-assignment instability of Section 4.4 breaking the same-motif requirement. **Suggested addition**: report the accuracy of a stated post-merge (e.g. merge all calls within N bp regardless of motif) on the maize arrays — coverage, boundary offset and fragmentation after merge — so a reader knows what one interval per array costs. If period-assignment drift is the cause, say so; it links W3 and the Section 4.4 limitation into one diagnosable problem rather than two independent ones.

4. **W4 — Sensitivity can only be set by re-running, and the paper never tests whether the per-call score would let a user filter instead.** The Introduction presents run-time-selectable sensitivity as the third demonstrated property, and Table 1d gives four operating points at 4.5–5.3 hours each. From an operations standpoint that is the expensive way to move along a curve: the cheap way is to emit the permissive output once and threshold a score in seconds. BWTandem computes a per-call quality score (Supplementary S1.1, Q = L(1−e)), but no analysis anywhere asks whether that score ranks true against false calls. If it does, a user recovers most of the curve from one run and the tool becomes far more attractive; if it does not, that is itself important, because it means the 21.9% unique calls of Section 4.4 cannot be triaged by any means the user has. **Suggested analysis, using outputs already produced**: take the BWTandem-H output and sweep a threshold on the reported score, plotting region precision against recall; overlay the four configuration points. Anything close to the configuration curve turns four expensive runs into one.

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit

- **Explicit assumptions**: The stated premise is that the binding constraint on de novo repeat discovery is the period-range parameter and its unpredictable cost (Section 1). Within the tools compared this holds and is well evidenced. But the premise is scoped to period-parameterised detectors, and the paper then generalises to "de novo genome annotation" (Section 4.1 heading) without engaging the annotation workflow's actual entry point, which is de novo repeat family discovery followed by library-based masking. The period bound is a real obstacle; the paper does not establish that it is the binding one for the annotation task it names.

- **Implicit assumptions**: Three go unstated. (i) That a repeat interval is the useful unit of output. For satellite work the useful unit is the array and, increasingly, the higher-order repeat structure within it; a BED of 253–475 calls per array is an intermediate, not a product (W3). (ii) That the period range and the sensitivity threshold are the parameters a user wants to control. The seventeen environment variables of Supplementary S2 say otherwise, and the more valuable control for an adopter is a post-hoc score threshold, which is not evaluated (W4, W5). (iii) That per-genome cost is the relevant cost. In pangenome and T2T work the unit is the assembly panel, and a footprint that scales at 8 GB per thread behaves differently there than the per-genome table suggests (W8).

- **Paradigmatic assumptions**: The paper inherits the benchmark-agreement paradigm of the tool-comparison genre: quality is agreement with a curated reference. That paradigm is in direct tension with this tool's own positioning, which is explicitly about the case where no curated reference exists. The manuscript sees the tension clearly (Section 4.1) and does not resolve it; the whole of W1 is a request to break the paradigm with one catalog-blind experiment. I would add that the paradigm also produces the recall-precision framing of Section 4.2, where "precision" against an incomplete catalog is not the quantity a downstream curator cares about — they care about how much curation effort a given output costs per true array recovered, which is a different and measurable thing (calls to review per array recovered).

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

- **Parallel work the manuscript does not engage**: The related-work set (TRF, mreps, tantan, ULTRA, NCRF, TRASH, TideHunter) is exclusively tandem-repeat *detectors*. The workflow slot BWTandem claims — annotation of a genome before any species-specific library exists — is currently occupied by the de novo repeat family discovery pipelines (RepeatModeler-class tools feeding RepeatMasker-class masking) and, for satellite structure specifically, by monomer-decomposition and HOR-inference tools. Those neighbours are not competitors on the benchmark, which is exactly why engaging them would strengthen rather than weaken the paper: BWTandem plausibly sits *upstream* of them, supplying candidate arrays that a family-inference step then organises. Stating that pipeline position explicitly in Section 4.1 would convert a vague "moves repeat discovery earlier" into a concrete adoption story, and would also make clear why family grouping being out of scope is acceptable rather than a gap.

- **Borrowing opportunities**: Two concepts from adjacent practice would help directly. First, *calibrated per-call confidence*, standard in variant calling, is what turns a 50%-precision callset from a liability into a resource (W4). Second, *curation cost per recovered array* — used routinely in annotation projects to choose tools — is a metric this paper could compute from data it already has, and would reframe Section 4.2's recall-over-precision argument in terms a curator can act on rather than in terms of an incomplete catalog.

- **Methodological borrowing**: The invariance testing common in assembly-evaluation work (does the answer change when you re-partition the input?) is the missing check in W2 and is cheap: split, run, compare. The pangenome community has converged on it precisely because parameterisation-by-sequence-length bugs are hard to see any other way.

#### Practical Impact

- **Real-world application**: There is a real job here. For a lab that has just finished a T2T assembly of a non-model organism and wants a same-day survey of what satellite families are present and where, BWTandem-P at 1–2,000 bp is a good answer, and 31 minutes on a 132 Mb genome makes it a survey you re-run rather than a job you schedule. The Arabidopsis result supports this directly. What does not yet follow is the stronger claim of Section 4.1: nothing shown establishes that the output supports the next step (grouping into families), and the paper says so.

- **Implementation feasibility**: The barriers, in the order I would hit them: seventeen environment variables with no preset (W5); a BED that needs a merge step of unspecified design before it describes arrays (W3); 22 GB per job, which excludes shared or laptop-class use and constrains node packing (W8); untested behaviour on fragmented assemblies, which is most non-model assemblies (W2); and invalid motif strings in a small fraction of calls that a naive parser will choke on (W9). None is fatal; all are the difference between a tool I can wrap and a tool I have to wrap carefully.

- **Stakeholders**: Two constituencies are under-served by the current framing. Clinical and population STR users are implicitly addressed by the microsatellite results but are the group least able to use fragmented output and unreliable period assignment, and they pay the satellite search whether they want it or not (W10). Non-model-organism groups — the paper's own target in Section 4.5 — typically work with the smallest compute allocations and the most fragmented assemblies, which is exactly the intersection of W2 and W8. A short "recommended usage" paragraph naming which configuration serves which constituency would go a long way.

#### Broader Implications

- **Ethical dimensions**: I see no ethics concerns specific to this work; it operates on public reference assemblies with no human-subject dimension. One indirect point: repeat annotations propagate into clinical STR pipelines, and a callset at ~50% precision entering such a pipeline without a filterable score is a real hazard. That is an argument for W4 rather than an ethics finding.

- **Social impact**: The equity-relevant consequence is compute accessibility. A tool needing 22 GB and 12 hours per human-scale genome is available to well-resourced groups; the non-model-organism labs the paper names in Section 4.5 are frequently not those groups. The one-thread 13.32 GB configuration mentioned in Section 4.4 partly addresses this and deserves to be named as a supported low-memory mode rather than appearing only as a scaling measurement.

- **Future directions**: The maize flank result (Section 3.3.3) is, from my seat, the most interesting thing in the paper, and the authors already frame the right follow-up — the same measurement across curated satellite catalogs in other species. I would prioritise it over further benchmark optimisation. If curated satellite boundaries are systematically conservative, that changes annotation practice across the T2T corpus, which is a larger contribution than a position on a recall-precision frontier.

### Questions for Authors

1. Can you show, on a genome you have already run, that a user with no catalog recovers the known families? Concretely: cluster the reported motifs from the maize or Arabidopsis run and report how many curated families appear among the top clusters, and how many comparable-sized clusters are spurious. Without this, Section 4.1's positioning rests on assertion, as you note yourself.
2. Is the output invariant to how the assembly is partitioned? Given the per-chromosome parameterisation and the 100 Mb discontinuity, what happens to the calls when a chromosome is split into contigs, or when the input is a scaffold-level assembly?
3. Does the per-call quality score rank true calls above false ones well enough that one permissive run plus a threshold reproduces the operating-point curve? If yes, the run-time-configuration framing understates the tool; if no, how should a user triage the 21.9% unique calls?
4. What does the tool do at its own defaults? A single row in Table 1a or 1d would tell an adopter what their first run produces, and would answer whether the benchmarked settings should simply become the defaults.
5. Would tantan at a 2,000 bp maximum period enter the 101–2,000 bp stratum? It is a 54-minute run, and it is the comparison that decides whether the wide-range capability is unique or merely unexercised in the competitors' default configurations.
6. What merge produces one interval per array, and what does the coverage and boundary accuracy look like after it? Since post-processing already merges across 100× the period, please say what defeats it — I suspect the period-assignment drift of Section 4.4, and if so the two limitations are one problem.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

I would argue the paper has the sign of its own result backwards.

Its central property is that BWTandem's cost barely moves with `--max-period`. But §3.1 concedes Tier 3 searches 100 bp–100 kb in *both* arms; §2.1.2 fixes Tier 1 at periods 1–9 (enumeration 1–6) irrespective of the parameter; §2.1.3 fixes the Tier-2 general scan at 10–50 bp; §2.1.5 fixes the gap-fill at 100–360 bp and the catch-all at 1–20 bp. The only component the parameter moves is the upper bound of Tier 2's long-unit phase. Flatness is therefore not a decoupling achieved by indexing — it is the arithmetic signature of a pipeline that performs one fixed workload and filters the report. Any architecture built that way is flat, indexed or not.

Restated correctly, the property is a **cost floor, not a saving**. BWTandem cannot be made cheap for a narrow search, and ULTRA's "bad" scaling is precisely its ability to charge 0.23 h when a user wants only hexamers (§1). The paper's own matched-range panel prices this. At `--max-period 100`, BWTandem-P spends 17.9 core-hours (4.5 h × 4 threads) to recover 54.69% of adotto at 62.17% precision; tantan spends 0.9 core-hours to recover 78.00% at 57.66%, in 0.27 GB against 29.21 GB (Tables 1a, 1d; manifest job 5982255). Most tandem-repeat users know their range — forensic panels, disease loci, a named satellite family. For them the headline property is a liability, and that user appears nowhere in this manuscript.

The wide-range user's demonstrated gain, meanwhile, is +1.42 percentage points of adotto regions above period 100 over capped TRF, at 65.97% precision against TRF's 97.18% (Table 1c), on a benchmark §1 concedes excludes the human centromeric satellite the design targets.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Raise tantan's ceiling.** One flag, one run, on a tool that finishes human in 0.9 h at 0.27 GB. This is the single most decisive missing experiment in the manuscript, because it is the only one that can test §1's novelty claim ("cost both low and predictably insensitive to the period range") against the cheapest comparator. If tantan at 500–2,000 bp is even 20× its current cost, it lands at ~18 core-hours against BWTandem's 24.2 with 1/80th the memory, and the cost claim is gone. If it is *not*, the claim survives — but only then. Not running it, while re-running two other tools for precisely this reason, is the paper's largest unforced omission (C3).

2. **Price the stratified two-tool workflow the one-pass argument displaces.** A user can run tantan for ≤100 bp (0.9 core-h, 78.00% region recall at 57.66% precision, 93.16%-precision alternative from capped TRF at ≤100 bp) and capped TRF for 100–500 bp (33.7 core-h, 2.02% / 97.18% in that stratum). Total ≈34.6 core-hours against BWTandem's 24.2, and the composite is far more precise in both strata. The paper never computes this comparison, though every input is in Tables 1a–1c. "One pass" is then a convenience worth ~10 core-hours, not a capability.

3. **Estimate the cost curve instead of assuming it is unknowable.** §1's motivating premise is that "a user cannot know in advance which case they are in." A user can: run each candidate tool at two ceilings on one chromosome (~1/20th of a genome) and read the slope in minutes. The manuscript's own maize numbers are exactly such a measurement. The problem the paper is built to remove has a cheap empirical solution it never mentions, which is a more parsimonious account of why the range parameter has not previously been treated as a crisis.

4. **Contribute the seeding as a component, not the pipeline as a product.** §4.3 observes that TRF "also derives candidate periods from the spacing of matching k-tuples before aligning," i.e. the FM-index is a drop-in replacement for one existing stage. A same-pipeline A/B (index seeding vs k-tuple seeding, everything else fixed) would isolate the mechanism the paper wants to claim, in one experiment, on one genome — and would be immune to C1 and C2. The whole-pipeline framing is the weaker of the two available designs and is not defended against the stronger one.

5. **A simpler explanation of the whole cost story.** BWTandem is fast on Arabidopsis because it does not align megabase satellite arrays copy-by-copy; it is flat in `--max-period` because almost nothing it does depends on that parameter. Neither of these requires the FM-index. "Avoid quadratic alignment on dense arrays, and fix your search windows" fits every cost observation in the paper with no appeal to compressed indexing, and is not tested against.

### Unexamined Premise

**The manuscript assumes the maximum-period parameter is a burden the user is forced to guess. It is more often a control the user wants.**

Every argument in §1 and §4.1 presupposes that the period range of the arrays a user is looking for is unknown, so that any bound is a gamble and any tool that removes the bound is doing the user a favour. But the parameter is not only a bound on what is *reported* — for ULTRA, TRF and mreps it is also a lever on what is *paid*, and a user who knows their range uses it to buy a cheap run. ULTRA's 0.23 h on maize at a 6 bp ceiling is not a pathology; it is the parameter working. BWTandem's architecture removes that lever, and §2.1.4 makes the removal structural: "the command-line maximum period filters its report rather than bounding its search."

Under the paper's premise, flat cost is a feature. Under the inverted premise, flat cost means every user pays the satellite-scan price whether or not they want satellites — and the paper's own Table 1d shows what that price is (17.9 core-hours for 54.69% recall at `--max-period 100`, against tantan's 0.9 core-hours for 78.00%). Neither premise is argued for; the first is simply assumed throughout, and the manuscript contains no run in which BWTandem is asked to be cheap. This premise is upstream of C1 and of the strongest counter-argument, and none of the eight challenge dimensions reaches it directly.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- **Objections the manuscript already answers — I raise and withdraw them.** (i) Corroboration circularity: answered in §4.2, and answered *against* the authors ("the corroborating tools are de novo detectors responding to the same periodicity signal, not independent evidence"; leave-one-out puts BWTandem last of three). (ii) Different FASTA on the two sides of the human comparison: answered in §2.2.2, with the ~5% direction stated. (iii) Executed-vs-released commit divergence: answered in Data and Code Availability, with the two behavioural differences and their sign. (iv) Non-ACGT motif calls: answered in §2.2.1, and the reported figures are the conservative ones. (v) Period-band sensitivity and array fragmentation: both stated as limitations against the tool in §4.4 and §3.3.2. (vi) Absence of CIs: stated outright in §2.2.3. None of these should be re-raised by the panel.
- The Table 1b caption's quantification of its own post-hoc-filter artefact ("54.2% have no overlapping native call at all") is a piece of methodological work most submissions omit, and it forecloses the obvious "you filtered rather than re-ran" objection cleanly.
- `results/README.md`'s explicit interpretability floor for cost differences is good practice. It is also, as M1 notes, the standard against which the paper's own headline ratio should be — and is not — judged. Publishing the standard and then not applying it to oneself is a narrower fault than not having one.

That gap between surfaces is where C1, M2 and M4 live, and closing it is largely a rewriting task rather than a re-analysis one — with the exceptions of C3 (one tantan run), C2 (one maize ablation) and M5 (one TRF run).
